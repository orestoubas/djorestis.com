# The marketing plan — DJ Orestis

Built for the real constraints you gave me: **3–5 hours a week** and **under €150 a month**.
Everything here fits that. Nothing assumes an agency retainer or an ad budget you don't have.

Every supporting document referenced below is in this folder and ready to use.

---

## The strategy in five sentences

You cannot outspend or out-rank the established Brussels DJ companies this year — one has
fifteen years of domain age and 150 reviews. So you win somewhere else: **own the Greek
niche completely**, because nobody else can credibly claim it; **get recommended by venues
and planners**, because that is how DJs actually get booked; **capture couples early**,
because weddings decide 6–18 months ahead and everyone else loses those people; **accumulate
reviews**, because that is the one ranking lever entirely within your control; and **convert
better than competitors** because your site is faster, multilingual and transparent about
price.

That is a defensible position. "Rank first for every DJ search in Belgium" is not.

## The one thing blocking everything

**You have no photographs.** Not on the site, not on the Business Profile, not for social,
not for ads. Every channel below performs worse without them, and the venue outreach
converts badly when a venue clicks through to a site with no images of you working.

**Do this first: book a photographer for one Papillon Schuman night. €150–300, once.**
It is the only spending I recommend right now, and it unblocks six channels simultaneously.
Shot list is in `PHOTOS.md`.

## The weekly rhythm (3–5 hours)

The whole plan reduces to five habits. Do these and the rest takes care of itself.

| When | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | 45 min | Send **5 venue/planner outreach emails** from `VENUE-OUTREACH-KIT.md`. Log them in the tracker. Follow up anything sent 10 days ago |
| **Wednesday** | 45 min | **One social post** from the rotation in `INSTAGRAM-PLAN.md`. Cross-post to TikTok |
| **After every event** | 10 min | **Ask for a review.** Everyone, not just the happy ones. Template in `GOOGLE-BUSINESS-PROFILE.md` |
| **Friday** | 30 min | Reply to enquiries and reviews. **Log where every enquiry came from** |
| **First Monday of the month** | 1 hour | Add 3–5 directory listings. Check Search Console → Performance → Queries. Write one blog post if you have time |

That is roughly 2.5–4 hours. The remainder is slack for the months when work is busy.

## Phase 1 — Months 1–2: build the foundation

**Goal: photos, ten reviews, and the free listings that establish you exist.**

- [ ] **Photo and video production day** — one Papillon night. Send me the files and I put
      them on the site the same day.
- [ ] **Review drip starts.** ~5/month. Begin with people you're still in touch with:
      Papillon Schuman, community organisers, recent couples, corporate contacts.
      Never screen for happy clients — that is review gating, and Google's penalty is
      removing *all* your reviews.
- [ ] **Free listings, Tier 1 and 2** — eventplanner.be, Infobel, Goudengids/Pagesdor,
      WEWED, Cueup, ShowBird, plus the Greek institutions (Greek Community of Brussels,
      Orthodox Metropolis, Schaerbeek parish, Belgo-Hellenic Chamber).
- [ ] **Register @djorestis** on Instagram and TikTok. Avatar and templates are in
      `social/`.
- [ ] **Bing Webmaster Tools** — see `SEARCH-ENGINE-SETUP.md`.
- [ ] **Brevo account** on the free tier. Send me the form endpoint and I switch on the
      lead-capture blocks already built into the site.
- [ ] **Ask soundsgreekevents.be** for a contextual in-content link. Not a footer link.

## Phase 2 — Months 2–4: relationships and capture

**Goal: get onto supplier lists, and stop losing couples who aren't ready yet.**

- [ ] **Venue outreach begins** — 5 emails a week from the 27 verified venues in
      `VENUE-OUTREACH-KIT.md`. Start with the five priority targets: Salon 58,
      Château de Petit-Leez, Château Sainte-Anne, La Tricoterie, and the planner
      Alina Vandaele.
- [ ] **Lead magnets go live** — the wedding guide and corporate playbook are written
      (`leadmagnets/`). Once Brevo is connected, the capture blocks appear on the wedding
      and corporate pages automatically, and the 9 nurture emails load straight in.
- [ ] **Testimonials** — as reviews arrive, send me three or four and they appear on the
      service pages.
- [ ] **First case study** — one corporate event, written as: the brief, what you did,
      what happened. This converts corporate planners better than any services list.
- [ ] **Formspree** on the contact form so enquiries submit in-page and become measurable.

## Phase 3 — Months 4–9: compound

**Goal: first organic rankings, and paid traffic that actually pays.**

- [ ] **Google Ads, Greek only, €3/day** — once photos and ~10 reviews are live.
      Exact-match keywords, full negative list, one ad group. Details in
      `ads/BUDGET-REVISION.md`.
- [ ] **YouTube channel** — set recordings and event clips. The most under-exploited
      channel available to you; correlates with AI-search visibility far more strongly
      than backlinks do.
- [ ] **Press pitch** — the Greek-diaspora-nightlife story to BRUZZ and The Brussels
      Times. Pitch the story, not the service.
- [ ] **Wedding fair** — an exhibitor listing plus couples in person, the
      highest-converting wedding channel there is.
- [ ] **Venue pages** — as you play new rooms, convert each event recap into a page led by
      the venue name. Couples search "[vendor] at [venue]" with the date already booked.

## Seasonality — push ahead of demand, not during it

| Months | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Jan–Apr** | Wedding booking season for the May–Sept summer. Heaviest wedding outreach and content |
| **May–Sept** | Wedding delivery season. Capture photos and reviews at every event. Low outreach |
| **Sept–Nov** | Corporate books December parties. Switch outreach to companies and agencies |
| **Dec** | Delivery month. Ask for reviews relentlessly. Plan January |

## What to measure

One number: **cost and source per enquiry.** Ask every person who contacts you how they
found you, and write it down. After six months that single habit tells you where to spend
your time — and it beats any dashboard.

Ignore impressions, click-through rate and follower counts. They do not pay.

## An honest closing note

Nothing here is clever. It is five habits done weekly for a year, plus one afternoon with a
photographer. The reason most DJs don't dominate their market isn't that they lack a
strategy — it's that they do outreach for three weeks and stop.

The Greek niche is genuinely yours to take, and the window is open because no one else in
Brussels is contesting it. That is the part I'd protect.
