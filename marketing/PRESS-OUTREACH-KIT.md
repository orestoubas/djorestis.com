## How to use this document

This is not a "send a press release to 200 outlets" plan. That fails for a working DJ with no news hook.

The plan is narrow and slow: **five story angles**, matched to **~20 outlets and ~10 audio shows**, pitched by one person at a rate of **3–5 emails a week**, with disciplined follow-ups. Expect most of them to say nothing. Expect two or three to say yes in a year. That is a normal, good outcome — and each one compounds into the next.

Order of operations:

1. Build the press page at `djorestis.com/press/` (Section 5). **Do this first.** Pitching without it wastes your best shots.
2. Pick the angle that matches the season (Section 3).
3. Pitch 3–5 outlets from Section 1–2 per week using the templates in Section 4.
4. Log everything in a simple sheet. Follow up once at day 7–10. Then stop.
5. Recycle every placement into the next pitch (Section 6).

**Budget reality:** everything here costs €0 except optional photography. Under €150/month covers a one-off photo session amortised over a few months, or a cheap media-tracking spreadsheet you build yourself. Do **not** buy a press-release distribution service. They do not work for this.

## 1. Belgian & European media targets

**Legend**
- **Realistic** — a normal, well-matched pitch has a genuine chance of a reply this year.
- **Ambitious** — you need a strong hook, a news peg, or an existing clipping to get in.
- **Long shot** — pitch it once, expect silence, do not build a plan around it.

**Contact rule used throughout:** only generic, publicly published editorial addresses are named below. Where no address could be verified, the entry says *find the current contact via the outlet's contact page* — do not guess at `firstname.lastname@` patterns and fire blind. A bounced or misaddressed email to a named person is worse than a correct email to a general desk.

### Brussels city media

**1. BRUZZ — realistic (highest priority target)**
- *Covers:* everything Brussels — city life, culture, nightlife, music, neighbourhoods, the city's communities. Weekly free magazine, website, radio 98.8 FM and TV. Explicitly interested in the diversity of who actually lives in Brussels.
- *Language:* Dutch (some English content at bruzz.be/en)
- *URL:* https://www.bruzz.be — contact page: https://www.bruzz.be/contacteer-bruzz
- *How to pitch:* general editorial `redactie@bruzz.be`; music desk `muziekredactie@bruzz.be`; general `info@bruzz.be`. There is also a news-tip form on the contact page and a WhatsApp tip line. Pitch the music desk for anything about the scene, the general desk for anything about community.
- *Why it fits:* the Greek-diaspora-nightlife angle and the restaurant-residency angle are both exactly BRUZZ's register. Dutch or English both land; they cover a Dutch-language brief in a multilingual city.

**2. BRUZZ Agenda / Uit in Brussel — realistic (listings, not features)**
- *Covers:* what's on in Brussels this week.
- *Language:* Dutch
- *URL:* https://www.bruzz.be
- *How to pitch:* submit dated public events through the agenda channels on the contact page. This is a listing, not coverage — but a listing puts your name in front of the same editors who commission features. Use it for Sounds Greek Events public nights and the Greek Food Festival.

**3. The Brussels Times — realistic**
- *Covers:* Belgian news in English, EU affairs, plus a features desk that runs culture, human interest, expat life, migration, equality and travel.
- *Language:* English
- *URL:* https://www.brusselstimes.com — about: https://www.brusselstimes.com/about-us
- *How to pitch:* general/editorial `info@brusselstimes.com`. They also run a **contributor / opinion writer form** at https://www.brusselstimes.com/contributors — note that opinion pieces are unpaid. Staff bylines are listed on the site with author pages; identify the current features editor there and address the pitch to the desk. **Do not guess an individual address** — send to `info@` and name the desk in the subject line.
- *Why it fits:* the biggest English-reading audience in Brussels, and the audience most likely to hire an English-speaking wedding and corporate DJ. This is your single best commercial fit.

**4. BX1 — realistic**
- *Covers:* the Brussels-region news channel in French — TV, radio, web. Strong on Brussels neighbourhoods, communities, and local culture.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://bx1.be — editorial/press releases: https://bx1.be/contacts/redaction-et-communiques-de-presse/
- *How to pitch:* `actu@bx1.be` is the published address for suggesting subjects to the newsroom; `info@bx1.be` is general. Pitch in French (use the FR template in Section 4).
- *Why it fits:* BX1 loves "a community you didn't know was here, in your city". The Greek Food Festival and the Greek community angle are made for it. Also: TV/radio appearances give you video assets you can reuse forever.

**5. The Bulletin (Ackroyd Publications) — realistic**
- *Covers:* the oldest English-language outlet in Belgium; lifestyle, culture, going out, expat practicalities.
- *Language:* English
- *URL:* https://www.thebulletin.be — publisher Ackroyd Publications, Cantersteen 47, 1000 Brussels
- *How to pitch:* `info@ackroyd.be` is the verified publisher address. Ask them to route to the right editor for a culture/going-out feature. Check thebulletin.be for a current editorial contact before sending.
- *Why it fits:* same expat readership as The Brussels Times, different editors. Pitch both — but with **different angles**, never the same one.

**6. Together Magazine — realistic / ambitious**
- *Covers:* English-language international lifestyle magazine for Brussels — fashion, lifestyle, leisure, people, going out. Roughly ten issues a year.
- *Language:* English
- *URL:* https://togethermag.eu
- *How to pitch:* `info@togethermedia.eu` (verified editorial/general address). Magazine lead times are long — pitch 8–12 weeks ahead of any seasonal hook.
- *Why it fits:* very high-affinity readership (international, social, event-going). Lower reach than the newspapers, but a warmer audience.

**7. Brussels' Kitchen — realistic (for the residency angle only)**
- *Covers:* the leading Brussels food/going-out guide — restaurants, bars, brunch, hotspots. Founded 2012 by Chloé Roose.
- *Language:* French and English
- *URL:* https://www.brusselskitchen.com — about: https://www.brusselskitchen.com/en/about/
- *How to pitch:* `info@brusselskitchen.com`. **Important:** they publicly state they do not accept invitations or collaborations in exchange for coverage. So never offer anything. Pitch the *story* of how a restaurant builds a music night, with Papillon Schuman as the case study, and let the venue be the subject rather than you.
- *Why it fits:* the four-year residency angle is a genuinely good food-media story about how restaurants program atmosphere.

**8. Bruxelles Secrète — realistic**
- *Covers:* Brussels curiosities, hidden places, what's on, city culture, high social reach.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://bruxellessecrete.com
- *How to pitch:* use the contact page on the site — no editorial address could be verified. Their format favours short, visual, "did you know Brussels has…" pieces, so pitch the *phenomenon* (a Greek nightlife circuit across Brussels, Vienna, Leuven and Lille), not the person.
- *Why it fits:* enormous local social reach; easy yes for a light cultural curiosity.

**9. agenda.brussels / visit.brussels Agenda — realistic (listings)**
- *Covers:* the official Brussels cultural agenda: concerts, festivals, nightlife.
- *Language:* FR / NL / EN
- *URL:* https://agenda.brussels — pro/organiser view: https://agenda.brussels/en/pro/events ; visit.brussels agenda: https://www.visit.brussels/en/visitors/agenda
- *How to pitch:* not a pitch — register as an organiser and submit public events via the organiser back office linked from the agenda site. Free. Do it for every public Sounds Greek Events date.
- *Why it fits:* it is the source journalists and listings editors scrape. Being in it is cheap discoverability and a legitimacy signal.

### Belgian national press & lifestyle

**10. Le Soir — ambitious**
- *Covers:* the leading French-language daily; culture and society pages.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://www.lesoir.be — newsroom: Rue Royale 100, 1000 Brussels
- *How to pitch:* Le Soir does not publish a single "pitch us" address. The right method is to read the culture/société pages, identify the journalist who already covers Brussels communities or nightlife, and contact them via the paper's contact page or their public professional profile. Pitch in French, in three short paragraphs, with a clear news peg.
- *Why it fits:* enormous credibility if it lands. Realistically, it lands only off the back of a festival or a genuine news moment.

**11. La Libre Belgique — ambitious**
- *Covers:* French-language daily; strong culture and society sections.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://www.lalibre.be
- *How to pitch:* via the contact page; same approach as Le Soir — target the specific journalist covering Brussels culture, not the paper.

**12. Moustique — ambitious**
- *Covers:* French-language weekly on current affairs, culture, TV, society. Long-form and personality-led.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://www.moustique.be
- *How to pitch:* via the contact page on the site. No editorial address verified.
- *Why it fits:* Moustique likes a portrait with a social angle. The "playing free for your own community" angle is the one to send here.

**13. Le Vif / Focus Vif (Roularta) — long shot**
- *Covers:* Le Vif is the French-language weekly news magazine; Focus Vif is its culture and music supplement.
- *Language:* French
- *URL:* https://www.levif.be — publisher: https://www.roularta.com/en/brands/le-vif/
- *How to pitch:* contact page only. Focus Vif covers music with a critical/scene lens — realistically it covers releases and festivals, not working DJs. Pitch only if you release music or curate a festival stage.

**14. Het Laatste Nieuws / Het Nieuwsblad — regional desks — ambitious**
- *Covers:* the two biggest Dutch-language dailies, both with regional desks (Brussel, Vlaams-Brabant/Leuven) that carry local human-interest stories.
- *Language:* Dutch
- *URL:* https://www.hln.be · https://www.nieuwsblad.be
- *How to pitch:* the national desks will ignore you; the **regional** desks are the realistic door. Find the current Brussels or Leuven regional contact via each paper's contact page. Local-news desks respond to a concrete, dated, local event — "a Greek night in Leuven this Saturday, the community has been doing this for X years" beats any career narrative.
- *Why it fits:* your Leuven and Brussels Greek community dates are exactly the local-colour item these desks need to fill.

**15. RTBF / VRT (public broadcasters) — long shot**
- *Covers:* national radio and TV, French and Dutch respectively.
- *URL:* https://www.rtbf.be · https://www.vrt.be
- *How to pitch:* via each broadcaster's contact page, or via a specific programme's own contact. Do not pitch "the RTBF" — pitch one named programme whose format you have actually listened to.
- *Reality check:* these come to you *after* BX1 or BRUZZ has covered you, not before.

### Event, wedding and music trade press

**16. Experience Magazine / eventnews.be — realistic**
- *Covers:* the Belgian events industry — agencies, venues, suppliers, trends. The trade press for the people who book you.
- *Language:* Dutch and French
- *URL:* https://eventnews.be
- *How to pitch:* via the contact page on eventnews.be. Pitch trade-useful content, not publicity: what event planners get wrong about music briefs, how multilingual audiences change a set, why a corporate crowd needs a different build than a wedding.
- *Why it fits:* the readership is literally your buyer. A trade byline is worth more commercially than a national newspaper mention.

**17. Kongres Magazine — ambitious**
- *Covers:* European meetings and events industry media — destinations, venues, trends.
- *Language:* English
- *URL:* https://kongres-magazine.eu
- *How to pitch:* contact page. Angle it as Brussels-as-a-destination: what an international corporate audience in Brussels expects from event music.

**18. Bruid & Bruidegom (Belgian edition) — realistic**
- *Covers:* Belgian wedding magazine, quarterly, with real-wedding features and supplier content.
- *Language:* Dutch
- *URL:* https://www.bruidenbruidegom.be
- *How to pitch:* via the contact page; they run a "real life wedding" strand, so the strongest route is to submit a *couple's* story (with the couple's written permission and the photographer's credited images) in which you are the DJ, rather than pitching yourself.
- *Why it fits:* highest commercial intent of any outlet in this document.

**19. Dansende Beren — realistic / ambitious**
- *Covers:* Belgian music site covering new music and concerts across the Dutch-language area. Volunteer-run.
- *Language:* Dutch
- *URL:* https://www.dansendeberen.be
- *How to pitch:* contact details are on the site; no address verified here. Being volunteer-run means slow replies and a preference for music (releases, mixes, scene reports) over service journalism. Pitch only with a mix, an edit series, or a scene piece.

### Greek diaspora media

**20. Ελληνική Κοινότητα Βρυξελλών / Hellenic Community of Brussels — realistic**
- *Covers:* announcements, events, culture and education for the Greek community of Brussels. Runs a newsletter and an announcements page.
