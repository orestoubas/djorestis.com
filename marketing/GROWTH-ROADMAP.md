## The three things blocking everything else

### 1. A production day — photo and video, one afternoon

This single action unblocks the website, social accounts, Google Business Profile, ads,
directory listings and the press kit simultaneously. Book a photographer *and* a videographer
for one of your Papillon Schuman nights. Budget €300–600 for both. Shot list:

- You at the decks, engaged, from three angles
- The crowd from behind the booth (the money shot — it proves the floor works)
- Detail: hands on the mixer, lighting rig, a full room
- One clean half-portrait for the About page and press kit
- 60–90 seconds of usable video: a wide static shot, a couple of close-ups, crowd energy

That yields: 15–20 site photos, a Business Profile gallery, a month of social content,
an aftermovie, and ad creative. Nothing else on this list is worth doing first.

### 2. Reviews — the compounding asset

Zero versus a competitor's 150. Crossing 10 produces a measured ranking lift, and reviews
are the first thing a couple or an event planner reads. Roughly five per month, asking
everyone rather than screening. Start with people you're still in touch with.

### 3. Video and a YouTube channel

The most under-exploited channel available to you. An Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands found
YouTube mentions correlate with AI-search visibility at r=0.737, against 0.218 for backlinks.
You have sets and event footage; the channel costs nothing but time, and every video becomes
a second search surface. Get consent before publishing footage of identifiable guests.

## What a professional agency would do that we haven't

### The revenue mechanics nobody thinks about

**A referral network beats SEO for a DJ.** This is how established DJs actually get booked:
venues, wedding planners, caterers and photographers recommending them. One venue putting
you on its preferred-supplier list is worth more than a year of blogging. Concretely: pick
fifteen Brussels wedding venues, send the press kit with a short personal note, and offer to
play one event at a reduced rate to get on the list. Same for the five or six wedding
planners working the Brussels market.

**Email capture, because weddings have a 6–18 month decision cycle.** Someone reading your
wedding page today is booking for next summer. Right now, if they aren't ready to enquire,
they are gone forever. A simple "planning guide" download in exchange for an email address,
then three or four useful emails over the following months, converts a meaningful share of
people your competitors lose. This is the single biggest hole in the current funnel.

**A follow-up system.** Most enquiries are lost to silence, not to price. Reply within a few
hours, follow up once after three days and again after ten. A spreadsheet is enough to start.

### Reputation and proof

**Testimonials on the site, not just on Google.** Quotes with a first name, event type and
city, on the relevant service page. Display them as visible text without review markup —
self-controlled review schema risks a manual penalty.

**Case studies.** Three detailed write-ups: one corporate, one Greek wedding, one community
event. Problem, what you did, what happened. These convert corporate planners far better
than a services list, and they slot into the press kit.

**Awards and wedding fairs.** Belgian wedding-industry awards are winnable when nobody in
your niche enters. Exhibiting at a fair gets you an exhibitor backlink, plus couples in
person — the highest-converting channel that exists for weddings.

### Earned media

**Pitch the story, not the service.** "Greek diaspora nightlife in Brussels" is a genuine
story for BRUZZ, The Brussels Times and Belgian lifestyle press. Unstructured editorial
mentions now count more in AI-search visibility than directory links. Journalists want a
narrative — Mykonos to the EU quarter, a community that dances together — not a DJ press
release.

**Podcast and radio.** Greek diaspora podcasts and Brussels community radio are accessible,
under-subscribed, and produce durable links and clips.

### Paid, done properly

**Retargeting.** Someone visits your wedding page and leaves. A €2/day retargeting campaign
keeps you visible for the months they spend deciding. Cheaper per conversion than search ads
and currently not running.

**Meta ads for weddings.** Couples are on Instagram, not searching Google, in the early
phase. Engaged-status targeting in Belgium is available and cheap. This wants photos first.

### Discipline

**A seasonal calendar.** Corporate books September–November for December parties. Weddings
book January–April for the May–September season. Push effort ahead of demand, not during it.

**Measure one number: cost per booking, by source.** Ask every client how they found you and
write it down. Within six months that single habit tells you where to spend, and it beats
any dashboard.

## About the word "dominant"

Worth being straight with you. You will not out-rank a competitor with fifteen years of
domain age and 150 reviews on "wedding DJ Brussels" this year — that is not a strategy
problem, it is arithmetic.

Dominance for a solo DJ looks different, and it is achievable:

- **Own the Greek niche completely** — every Greek search in Belgium, every community
  association, every Orthodox parish. Nobody can copy your position there.
- **Own a referral network** — fifteen venues and five planners who recommend you by name.
  Invisible to competitors and to Google, and it is where the reliable bookings come from.
- **Be the obvious corporate choice** — because you have case studies and a press kit while
  competitors have a phone number.

That is a defensible business. "Rank first for every DJ query in Belgium" is not, and chasing
it would waste your money.
