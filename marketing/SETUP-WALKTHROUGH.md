# PART 1 — Google Business Profile

Roughly 45 minutes, plus a verification wait.

## Step 1 — Create the profile

1. Go to **https://business.google.com** and sign in with the Google account you want to
   own this permanently (the same one holding Search Console and Analytics is ideal).
2. Click **Add your business** → **Add single business**.
3. **Business name:** type exactly

   ```
   DJ Orestis
   ```

   Nothing else. Do **not** write "DJ Orestis Brussels Wedding DJ" — keyword stuffing the
   name does work, and it triggered a wave of suspensions in 2026. Not worth it.

4. **Business category** (this is a real ranking factor — be precise, not broad):

   ```
   DJ
   ```

   If Google offers "Disc jockey" instead, take that. Do not pick "Entertainer" as primary.

5. Click **Next**.

## Step 2 — The location question (get this right)

1. *"Do you want to add a location customers can visit, like a store or office?"* → **No**.
   Clients do not come to you.
2. *"Where do you serve customers?"* → add your service areas. Type each and select it:

   ```
   Brussels
   Ixelles
   Schaerbeek
   Uccle
   Woluwe-Saint-Lambert
   Leuven
   Waterloo
   Wavre
   Antwerp
   Ghent
   Mechelen
   Namur
   Liège
   Charleroi
   Bruges
   Flemish Brabant
   Walloon Brabant
   ```

   **Do not add** the Netherlands, France, Germany, the UK or Greece. Google weights local
   proximity heavily; a service area spanning six countries dilutes your Brussels relevance,
   which is where the bookings are. The website carries the international message instead.

3. **Business address** — Google will still ask for one, for verification only. Enter your
   **real** address. Make sure the box that hides the address from the public profile stays
   ticked. Never use a fake address or a virtual office: that is the fastest route to a
   suspension, and reinstatement is slow and painful.

## Step 3 — Contact details

```
Phone:    your real number (see the note below)
Website:  https://djorestis.com
```

On the phone number: Google strongly prefers a working phone, and profiles without one can
look less complete. If you would rather not publish your personal mobile, get the prepaid
SIM before finishing this step. A profile with no phone at all is weaker than one with a
number you screen.

## Step 4 — Verification

This is the step most likely to give trouble, because a brand-new, address-hidden,
home-based entertainment business is exactly the shape Google scrutinises. You will most
likely get **video verification**.

**How to pass it first time.** Google asks for a single continuous recording — no cuts, no
stopping. Before you start, plan the shot:

1. **Start outside**, filming a street sign or a recognisable local landmark near your
   address, so location is evident.
2. **Walk to your door** and inside without stopping the recording.
3. **Show your equipment** — controller, speakers, lighting, cases. This proves the business
   is real and matches the category.
4. **Show proof of the business**: an invoice with your company name and VAT number
   (Orestis Vasileiadis, BE 0785.520.639), a bank letter or a contract.
5. **Show yourself** and, if you can, perform a live management action — open the Google
   Business Profile app on camera and show you can edit the profile.

Keep it to 30–60 seconds, well lit, steady, one take.

If verification fails twice, **request a manual review** rather than trying a third video —
repeated failed attempts make things worse.

## Step 7 — Services (the highest-evidence ranking lever — do not skip)

Controlled tests show detailed Services entries move rankings, with effect visible in
24–72 hours. Most competitors leave this empty.

### Finding the Services editor

Google retired the old `business.google.com` dashboard for single-location businesses. The
profile is now managed **inside Google Search**, and Services is easy to miss:

1. Signed in with the owner account, go to **google.com** and search **`my business`**
   (or your business name). The management panel appears at the top of the results.
2. Click **Edit profile**. Tabs appear: *About · Contact · Location · Hours · More*.
3. **Services is one of those tabs**, but it is frequently pushed off-screen — scroll the
   tab row sideways or widen the browser window.

**Shortcut:** while signed in, type **`edit services`** straight into the Google search bar.
It opens the editor directly. The same works for `edit business description` and `add photos`.

**On mobile:** Google Maps app → profile picture → *Your Business Profiles* → select it →
*Edit profile* → *Services*.

### If the Services tab is not there at all

- **Most likely the primary category.** Services is enabled per category. If the primary
  category ended up as "Entertainer" or "Event planner", change it to **DJ**
  (*Edit profile → About → Business category*). Worth doing regardless — a precise primary
  category is itself a proven ranking lever.
- **Or it has not propagated.** Verification completing and every feature unlocking are not
  simultaneous. Wait 24–48 hours after verification and check again.

## Step 8 — Attributes, hours, images

**Attributes** — tick everything true: *Identifies as LGBTQ+ friendly* if you wish,
*Online appointments*, *Onsite services*, and any language attributes offered.

**Hours** — you are not a shop. Set hours you will actually answer the phone, e.g.
Monday–Friday 10:00–20:00, Saturday 10:00–18:00. Do not claim 24/7.

**Logo:** upload `marketing/social/avatar-1024.png`
**Cover:** upload `marketing/social/gbp-cover-1200x675.png`

Add real event photos as soon as you have them. Note: controlled tests show photos do
**not** lift rankings — but they strongly affect whether someone clicks and enquires. Do it
for conversion.

## Step 9 — Reviews (the second proven ranking lever)

Crossing **10 reviews** produces a measurable ranking lift. You currently have zero, so this
is the single biggest gap between you and competitors sitting at 150.

1. In the profile dashboard, find **Ask for reviews** and copy your review link.
2. Send it the day after each event, while the client is still glowing.
3. Message to send:

   > Hi [name], it was a pleasure playing at your [wedding / company party] on [date] —
   > thank you for having me. If you enjoyed the night, a short Google review would mean a
   > lot and helps other people find me: [review link]

4. Start with people you are still in touch with: Papillon Schuman, community organisers,
   recent couples, corporate contacts.

**Three rules that matter more than the speed:**

- **Ask everyone, not only the happy ones.** Screening before you ask is "review gating".
  Google's penalty is removal of *all* your reviews, not just the gated ones, and it is now
  illegal in the US with FTC fines per violation.
- **Roughly five per month.** Requesting forty at once from your back catalogue is the worst
  possible move — a spike on a new profile is filtered hardest.
- **Never pay for reviews or write them yourself.** Pattern detection is good, and the
  profile can be penalised.

Reply to every review, positive or negative. Prospective clients read the replies.

## What NOT to bother with

Controlled testing found **no ranking effect** from Google Posts (441 keywords tracked over
9 weeks), from photos, or from geotagging photos — Google strips EXIF data. Post updates if
you enjoy it as marketing, but it is not SEO. Spend the time on Services and reviews.

# PART 2 — Bing Webmaster Tools

10 minutes. Worth doing because **ChatGPT Search retrieves through Bing's index** — if Bing
has not indexed a page, ChatGPT cannot cite it when someone asks for a DJ in Brussels.

## Step 1 — Sign in and import

1. Go to **https://www.bing.com/webmasters**
2. Click **Sign in** → choose **Sign in with Google** and use the same Google account that
   holds Search Console. This is the fast path.
3. On the welcome screen choose **Import from Google Search Console**.
4. Authorise access → select **djorestis.com** from the list → **Import**.

That carries over the site and its verification. No DNS record needed.

**If the import fails**, add the site manually:
- Click **Add a site**, enter `https://djorestis.com`
- Choose the **CNAME** verification option
- Bing gives you a host and a value — add them at Papaki exactly the way you added the
  Google verification record, then click **Verify**

## Step 2 — Submit the sitemap

1. Left menu → **Sitemaps**
2. Click **Submit sitemap** and paste:

   ```
   https://djorestis.com/sitemap.xml
   ```

3. Submit. It should report 136 URLs discovered.

## Step 3 — Ask for your priority pages to be indexed

Left menu → **URL Inspection**. Inspect each of these, then click **Request indexing**:

```
https://djorestis.com/
https://djorestis.com/greek-dj-brussels/
https://djorestis.com/wedding-dj-brussels/
https://djorestis.com/corporate-event-dj-brussels/
```

Bing crawls small new sites slowly on its own, so this is the difference between days and
months.

## Step 4 — IndexNow (2 minutes, big payoff)

IndexNow notifies search engines the instant a page changes, instead of waiting for a crawl.
Bing, Yandex, Seznam and Naver support it.

1. Left menu → **IndexNow**
2. Click **Generate API key**
3. **Send me the key.** I will add the key file to the site and wire the ping into the build,
   so every future change notifies Bing automatically.

Google does not use IndexNow — for Google, the sitemap and normal crawling are the route.

## Step 5 — Bing Places (separate from Webmaster Tools)

1. Go to **https://www.bingplaces.com** and sign in
2. Choose **Import from Google Business Profile** — Bing can pull everything across once your
   Google profile is verified, so **do this after Part 1**
3. Confirm the imported details and submit

# Order of operations

1. **Google Business Profile** — create, verify, description, categories, all 9 services
2. While waiting for verification: **Bing Webmaster Tools** — import, sitemap, IndexNow key
3. Once Google is verified: **Bing Places** import, then **Apple Business Connect**
   (businessconnect.apple.com, same service-area setup, free, almost no local competitor
   has claimed theirs)
4. Then start the review drip — five a month, everyone, no screening

## What to check, and when

**Weekly for the first month:** has the profile been verified? Any review to reply to?

**Monthly:** in Search Console, look at **Performance → Queries** — the actual words people
typed to find you. That is real data and should steer future content, unlike keyword guesses.

Ignore impressions and click-through rate as success measures. The number that matters is
enquiries, and the question to ask every new client is simply how they found you.
