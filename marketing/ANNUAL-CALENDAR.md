# The 12-month calendar

One page. What to do each month, why that month, and which document to open.
Sized for **3–5 hours a week** and **no recurring budget**.

The logic underneath: **push effort ahead of demand, not during it.** Couples book
January–April for a May–September wedding. Companies book September–November for December
parties. If you market during the season you are already too late.

---

## The five habits (every week, all year)

These never change. Everything below is layered on top.

| When | Time | What | Document |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 45 min | 5 outreach emails + follow up anything sent 10 days ago | `VENUE-OUTREACH-KIT.md` |
| Wed | 45 min | 1 social post, cross-posted to TikTok | `INSTAGRAM-PLAN.md` |
| After each event | 10 min | Ask for a review. Everyone, not just the happy ones | `GOOGLE-BUSINESS-PROFILE.md` |
| Fri | 30 min | Reply to enquiries and reviews. **Log where every enquiry came from** | — |
| 1st Mon of month | 1 hour | 3–5 new listings, check Search Console queries | `BACKLINKS-ACTION-PLAN.md` |

## Quarter 1 — January to March · **Wedding booking season**

This is the heaviest wedding-demand quarter of the year. Couples engaged over Christmas
start booking suppliers now for the summer.

**January**
- Wedding outreach at full weight — venues and planners, wedding-focused messaging
- Push the wedding guide page in every social post and email signature
- Apply to a spring wedding fair (exhibitor listing = backlink + couples in person)
- Review target: 3 this month

**February**
- Continue venue outreach; begin following up December's non-responders
- Publish one blog post on a wedding topic
- If photos exist: refresh the site and Business Profile galleries
- Valentine's week is a natural social moment for wedding content

**March**
- Last high-intent month before the season starts
- Chase every warm venue lead to a decision
- Confirm summer availability publicly ("2027 dates now open")

---

## Quarter 2 — April to June · **Season starts, capture everything**

**April**
- Final wedding push before delivery season
- Switch outreach emphasis toward restaurants and venues for summer terrace nights
- Prepare Greek-community summer events

**May**
- Wedding season begins. **Outreach drops to 2–3 emails a week** — protect your energy
- At every single event: capture photos and video, and ask for the review the next day
- Reviews are the whole job this quarter

**June**
- Peak delivery. Same discipline: capture, ask, log
- Publish one event recap as a venue-led page for each new room you play

---

## Quarter 3 — July to September · **Summer, then the corporate pivot**

**July**
- Mykonos and summer travel. Content writes itself — post from the road
- Light outreach; this is a quiet month for Belgian bookings

**August**
- Belgium is largely on holiday. Use the quiet for the work you never get to:
  case studies, site updates, the press pitches from `PRESS-OUTREACH-KIT.md`
- Prepare everything for September

**September** ⚠️ **The most important month of your year**
- **Corporate booking season opens.** Companies decide their December party now
- Switch all outreach to companies, event agencies and corporate venues
- Push the corporate playbook page hard
- If you run ads at any point, this is when corporate spend is worth it

---

## Quarter 4 — October to December · **Corporate delivery + next year's setup**

**October**
- Corporate outreach continues at full weight; most December decisions land this month
- Confirm December availability publicly — scarcity is real and honest here

**November**
- Last corporate window. Chase every warm lead
- Begin preparing January's wedding campaign so it launches on the 2nd

**December**
- Delivery month. Play, capture, and **ask for reviews relentlessly** — this is your
  highest-volume month for events, so it is your highest-volume month for reviews
- Between events: write next year's plan, review what worked

---

## The annual review (one afternoon, late December)

Open the enquiry log and answer four questions:

1. **Where did bookings actually come from?** Venue referrals, Google, community, social,
   word of mouth. Rank them.
2. **What did each channel cost in time or money?**
3. **Which service line paid best per hour worked?**
4. **What will you stop doing?** This is the important one. Most plans fail from
   accumulation, not from missing tactics.

Then rewrite the year ahead around the top two channels only.

## Milestones worth holding yourself to

| By when | Target |
|---|---|
| End of month 1 | Photos on the site · 3 reviews · 10 free listings live |
| End of month 3 | 10 reviews · 20 venues contacted · email capture running |
| End of month 6 | 1–2 venue supplier lists · first organic rankings on Greek queries · 1 case study |
| End of month 12 | 25+ reviews · 3–5 referral partners · a known cost per booking by source |

If month 3 arrives and you have fewer than 10 reviews, stop everything else and fix that
first. It is the highest-leverage number on this page.
