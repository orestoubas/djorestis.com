## 8. Does directory/citation building still work in 2026? — the evidence

**Short answer: yes, but the value has shifted from "links" to (a) NAP verification and (b) AI-search visibility. Quality and topical relevance are now everything; volume is worthless or harmful.**

### 8.1 Classic local pack / Maps

- **Whitespark, *2026 Local Search Ranking Factors*** — citations **lost roughly half a percentage point** versus the previous edition, continuing a slow multi-year decline, but remain a **significant input** in Google's local algorithm. Citation signals influence **~6–7% of local pack and organic results**. Their function is verification: telling Google the business is real, established and operating in a specific place. → https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/
- Consistently reported across 2026 commentary: **citation quality now matters far more than raw quantity.** (Local Falcon, Advice Local, 12AM Agency, OnlineCrib — all 2026 write-ups referencing the same Whitespark dataset.)

### 8.2 AI search (the big 2026 change)

- Per Whitespark's 2026 report, **3 of the top 5 AI-search visibility factors are citation factors**:
  1. presence on **expert-curated "best of" lists**,
  2. **prominence on top industry-relevant domains**,
  3. **quality/authority of unstructured citations** — newspaper articles, blog posts, government sites and **industry associations**.
- **Implication for this client, and it is a big one:** getting onto *"Best wedding DJs in Brussels"* lists, being prominent on **eventplanner.be / House of Weddings / WEWED**, and earning mentions from **BRUZZ, The Brussels Times, the Greek community associations and the Metropolis of Belgium** is now measurably more valuable than any number of generic directory submissions.

### 8.3 What actively hurts

- Google's **SpamBrain** has evolved to the point where it typically **nullifies** low-quality links rather than penalising — but Penguin/Panda are folded into the core algorithm and re-evaluate continuously, so bad signals can still contribute to a low-quality link-profile assessment.
- "Submitting to hundreds of generic directories produces negligible positive effect and contributes to a low-quality link-profile signal."
- Consensus framing: *ten high-quality links earned over three months will outperform a hundred low-quality links acquired in a week — and carry less risk.*
- Sources: https://editorial.link/directory-link-building/ · https://www.icopify.com/link-building/buying-link-building-services/googles-link-spam-policy/ · https://bluetree.digital/unnatural-links/ · Google's own link-spam policy in Search Essentials.

### 8.4 Operating rules for this project

1. **NAP must be byte-identical everywhere** — same business name string, same address format, same phone, same URL. Inconsistency is the actual risk, not the directories themselves.
2. **Three languages, one NAP.** Belgium's NL/FR split means duplicate listings on twin sites (Goudengids/Pagesdor). Keep the NAP identical even when the description is translated.
3. **Never buy bulk citation packages.** Use vendors' published lists as *research*; submit manually.
4. **Prioritise topical + geographic relevance** over domain authority.
5. **Budget by lead value, not by link value.** A €1,800 wedding-portal subscription must be judged on booked weddings, never on SEO.
