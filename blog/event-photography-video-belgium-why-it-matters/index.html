How important are photo and video for your event?

The music fades at midnight; the images are what your event becomes afterwards, here is my take on when they are worth it.

I am a DJ, so you might expect me to say the music is the most important thing at your event. Here is my view after years behind the decks: the music makes the night, but the photos and video are what the night becomes. Six months later, nobody can hum the third song of your opening set. Everybody is still looking at the pictures.

Why I started offering photo and video at all

It happened out of frustration. I kept playing beautiful weddings and lively corporate receptions where the emotional peaks, the surprise dance, the CEO pulled onto the floor, the yiayia leading a kalamatianó: went completely uncaptured, because the photographer had left after dinner or was never booked. The DJ knows exactly when these moments happen; we practically schedule them. So it made sense to build full packages where the person shaping the moments and the people capturing them work as one team.

What changes when the team is coordinated

  • No missed peaks. I know the first dance starts in ninety seconds; the videographer knows it too, because we share a timeline and I cue them. No sprinting across the room with a camera.
  • Better light in every shot. When lighting and photography come from one team, the lights are set for cameras, not against them. Dance-floor photos stop being a red-and-green blur.
  • One conversation for you. One briefing, one contract, one team that has worked together before. Planning fatigue is real, especially for couples organising a wedding in Belgium from abroad.

When photo and video are essential

In my experience, three types of events regret skipping professional coverage most:

  1. Weddings and baptisms. These happen once. Phone photos from guests are lovely as a bonus and heartbreaking as the only record. If budget forces a choice, take fewer hours of professional coverage rather than none, ceremony and first hours of the party are the core.
  2. Corporate events with an audience beyond the room. A company party is internal culture; the aftermovie is employer branding. Firms in Brussels increasingly book event coverage specifically for LinkedIn and recruitment. A two-minute video of a genuinely great party says more than any careers page.
  3. Milestone parties. A 50th birthday, a 25th anniversary, a graduation. The guest list at these events: all generations, people who rarely gather, often never assembles again in one room. That alone justifies a photographer.

When you can skip it

I will not pretend everything needs a camera crew. A casual team drink, a small house party, a regular company Friday bar, enjoy them and let phones do the work. Professional coverage earns its cost when the event is emotionally or professionally significant, and when the people in the room matter more than the occasion itself. If you are unsure which side your event falls on, ask yourself one question: in five years, will anyone wish they could see this night again? If the answer is yes, book the camera.

Questions to ask any photo/video provider

  • How many hours are covered, and what happens if the timeline overruns?
  • When do we receive the material, and in what form: full gallery, highlight film, both?
  • Who owns the images, and can we use them freely?
  • Have you worked with the DJ or venue before? Coordination is half the quality.

My bottom line

Music is the heartbeat of the night; images are its memory. The events I am proudest of are the ones where both were planned together from day one. If you are weighing up event photography and video in Belgium for a wedding, baptism or company party, tell me about it via my contact page. I will give you a straight answer about what your event does and does not need.
