How far in advance should you book your DJ?

A look inside a working DJ's calendar — which dates vanish a year out, and when a last-minute booking is genuinely fine.

Every autumn my inbox fills with two kinds of messages: couples booking weddings eighteen months away, and companies asking if I am free "some Friday in December" — three weeks before December. One of these groups gets their first-choice date. Let me open my calendar logic so you can be in that group, whatever you are planning.

The shape of a DJ's year in Belgium

Demand here is fiercely seasonal, and it drives everything about availability:

  • May to September: wedding season. Saturdays are the battlefield — there are only about twenty of them in the season, and every couple in Belgium wants one.
  • Late November to mid-December: the corporate rush. Nearly every company wants its year-end party in the same three-week window, overwhelmingly on Thursdays and Fridays. In my experience this is, hour for hour, the most oversubscribed stretch of the entire year.
  • January to April and October: breathing room. Baptisms, birthdays, community parties, corporate kick-offs — good availability, often better flexibility on everything.

Weddings: 9–14 months for a peak Saturday

For a Saturday wedding between May and September, I honestly recommend booking your DJ 9 to 14 months out — roughly when you book the venue. Established wedding DJs in Brussels see peak Saturdays go first, and by January, the coming summer's Saturdays are already thinning fast. Three practical escapes if you are late:

  1. Consider a Friday or Sunday. Availability roughly doubles, and many suppliers price more gently.
  2. Consider the shoulder months. A late-April or early-October wedding gets peak-quality suppliers with off-peak calendars.
  3. Ask anyway. Cancellations and date changes happen; I have played wonderful weddings booked eight weeks out because a date reopened.

One more Brussels-specific note: mixed-nationality couples often plan from abroad and fly in for a single planning weekend. If that is you, book the DJ early enough that your one Brussels weekend can include our meeting — the briefing matters more than the booking date.

Corporate events: book December in September

If your company party is in the year-end window, treat September as your deadline for locking the DJ — earlier if you want a specific Friday. The maths is unforgiving: hundreds of Brussels companies, a handful of prime dates, and every professional corporate event DJ fully booked weeks before the first mulled wine. Two workarounds smart organisers use: hold your party in January as a "new year party" — same budget buys better venues, suppliers and menus — or pick a Thursday, which has quietly become the corporate favourite anyway.

For corporate events outside the December window, 6–10 weeks ahead is usually comfortable.

Private parties, baptisms, community events

More forgiving: 2–3 months ahead is typically plenty, except when your date collides with wedding-season Saturdays. Greek community events in Belgium have their own hot dates too — around major holidays and festival weekends, the diaspora calendar concentrates just like the corporate one. If your celebration touches one of those, think like a December company: book early.

The quick reference

  • Peak-season Saturday wedding: 9–14 months ahead.
  • Off-peak or weekday wedding: 4–8 months.
  • December corporate party: lock it by September.
  • Other corporate events: 6–10 weeks.
  • Private parties: 2–3 months.
  • Any date, any time: asking costs nothing — calendars change.

And if you are reading this late

Do not panic-book the first available name. A rushed booking with the wrong DJ is worse than a calm search that lands on a Friday instead. Check availability first, fit second — but check both properly. My calendar and I are one message away via the contact page: send your date and I will tell you straight away if it is free, and if not, what I would do in your place.
