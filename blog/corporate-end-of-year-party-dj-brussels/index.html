From reception mode to midnight mode

A recap of an end-of-year celebration for an international firm in Brussels, and what corporate crowds really want from a DJ.

December in Brussels is corporate-party season, and last week I had the pleasure of DJing the end-of-year reception of an international firm here in the city. I have played for major international firms, including names in law and consulting, and while I keep client details private as a matter of professionalism, the shape of a great corporate night is something I can share.

The brief: elegant first, electric later

The event team's brief was one I hear often: "We want it classy, but we also want people to actually dance." Those two goals are a sequence, not a conflict. The evening opened with a standing reception: champagne, speeches, awards for the year's achievements. My role in that first hour is closer to an interior designer than a DJ: warm, low-profile grooves that make the room feel expensive and conversation feel easy.

Reading a room full of colleagues

A corporate crowd is the most honest crowd there is. Nobody bought a ticket; they are here with their colleagues and, crucially, their managers. That means the DJ has to remove every excuse not to dance. My method, refined over years as a corporate event DJ in Brussels:

  • Start with recognition, not surprise. The first dance-floor tracks must be songs everyone knows, the point is permission, not discovery.
  • Find the connectors. Every office has three or four people whose dancing gives everyone else the green light. Play for them first.
  • Respect the international mix. This firm's team spanned a dozen nationalities. A verse of Italian pop here, a French classic there, a Latin run for the Madrid office, small gestures that tell people they were seen.

The turn

Around 22:30 it happened, as it always does if you have built the night correctly: jackets came off, a partner was pulled onto the floor by their own team, and the room stopped being an office. From there my job was simply not to break the spell: mixing decades and genres, holding energy without exhausting it, and landing the final song at exactly the right emotional altitude.

The best compliment at a corporate party is not "great music". It is a director saying, "I have never seen this team like this."

Why this work matters

An end-of-year party is the one night a company thanks its people. When it works, it does more for team spirit than any away-day. That is why I treat the technical side: discreet setup, impeccable sound at speech volume and at party volume, coordination with caterers and AV, as seriously as the music itself. Many clients take the full package with sound and lighting so there is one point of contact for everything.
