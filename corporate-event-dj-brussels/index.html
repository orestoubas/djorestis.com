An English-speaking DJ for an international city

Brussels does not throw ordinary company parties. The room is usually a mix of nationalities who met at work: EU institutions, international companies, law firms, consultancies, federations and associations, with a dozen mother tongues between them and English as the shared one. That changes what the evening needs. A set that only works for one nationality leaves most of the room standing at the bar, and a host who cannot address the guests in a language they all follow slows the night down every time the microphone comes out.

I am a Brussels-based DJ with more than ten years of events behind me, in Belgium, France, Luxembourg, Greece and Austria, plus three full seasons on Mykonos and four years as resident DJ at Papillon Schuman here in Brussels. I work in English, Greek and Bulgarian, including announcements at the microphone, and written enquiries in French and Dutch are no problem at all.

  • English first, by default. Announcements, introductions and the handover to your speakers, in the language the whole room shares.
  • Discretion and polish. Appropriate dress, early and quiet setup, professional conduct with your guests and your leadership.
  • Programme awareness. Speeches, awards and surprises land on time; the music breathes around your agenda, never over it.
  • Genuinely international range. Electronic and house, Afro, Latin, RnB, 80s to today, and Greek, Balkan and Bulgarian when the room calls for it.

A corporate evening is two different jobs in one night

Almost every company event I play has two halves, and they ask for opposite instincts. Booking a DJ who is good at one of them is the most common way a corporate night goes flat.

Before the plates leave

Arrival drinks, dinner, the CEO's ten minutes, the awards, the video that has to be heard at the back of the room. Here the music is architecture, not entertainment: instrumental and vocal programming that holds a warm, premium atmosphere at a volume where two people across a table can talk without leaning in. Levels come down before every speech and come back up after, without anyone noticing the hand on the fader.

After the plates leave

The twenty minutes after dessert decide whether you have a party or a slow goodbye. I read who is still in the room, which nationalities are sitting together and who is already standing, and I build from familiar to physical: recognisable floor-fillers first, then the set deepens as the floor commits. Nobody is asked to dance. The floor simply fills, and then it stays full.

Between the two sits the part you never see on an invoice: a written running order agreed with you in advance, cross-checked with the venue and the caterer, with slack in it, because corporate dinners run late far more often than they run early.

Frequently asked questions

Are you an English-speaking DJ?

Yes. English is my working language for corporate events in Brussels, including announcements and introductions at the microphone. I also work in Greek and Bulgarian, and written enquiries in French or Dutch are welcome.

Do you provide sound and lighting equipment for corporate events?

Yes. Speakers, microphones and lighting can come under the same agreement as the DJ set, for events up to 500 guests, so there is no separate AV supplier to hire or coordinate. If your venue has an installed system or its own AV partner, I can work with theirs instead.

How do you invoice, and can you put our purchase order number on it?

I invoice as a Belgian VAT-registered business and your PO number, cost centre or internal reference goes on the invoice. Payment is by bank transfer on normal commercial terms, before or after the event, whichever your finance process requires.

Are you VAT registered?

Yes, VAT BE 0785.520.639. Prices on this page are quoted excluding VAT, and Belgian VAT is added on the invoice, so companies that recover it see the net cost straight away.

What does a corporate event cost?

A DJ set starts at €600 excl. VAT. With speakers, microphones and lighting, a full package typically lands around €1,000 depending on headcount, room and hours. You get a written quote before anything is confirmed.

Can you handle the microphone for speeches and awards?

Yes, that is part of the job. Wired or wireless microphones for your CEO, host or award presenters, levels checked with the actual speakers before guests arrive, music ducked and brought back around each slot, and walk-on music per winner if you want it.

How much time do you need for set-up and breakdown?

Two to three hours before doors for a full set-up, less when I am using the venue's installed system. Breakdown takes about an hour after the last track. I am also happy to set up in the morning for an evening event when the venue allows it, so there is nothing happening in the room while your guests are arriving.

How early do you arrive?

Well before your guests. For a standard evening I am on site two to three hours ahead, set up and sound-checked before the first arrival, and I stay in the room through the reception rather than appearing at dessert.

Can you work alongside our AV company or the venue technician?

Routinely. I come with my own controller and can take a feed into the house system, or run my own speakers alongside the conference rig. Tell me who the technician is and I will contact them directly about the patch, power and timings, so your team is not relaying technical questions.

What about decibel limits at our venue?

Many Belgian venues have a legal noise limit and some have a limiter on the stage box. Tell me which band applies, or who to ask, and the set is designed for it: tighter speaker placement, more low-end control and more use of recognisable vocals, which makes a room feel loud at a level the limiter accepts. I would rather plan for the limit than fight it in front of your guests.

Do you bring backup equipment?

Yes. A second controller, a spare laptop with the full library offline, duplicate cabling and a backup audio source travel to every corporate event. The music does not depend on wifi, a streaming service or one piece of hardware staying alive.

Can you play for an international, mixed-nationality audience?

That is my speciality. Electronic and house, Afro, Latin, RnB and 80s to today, plus Greek, Balkan and Bulgarian when the room wants it, adapted in real time, which is exactly what international teams in Brussels need.

Do you travel outside Brussels for business events?

Yes. Anywhere in Belgium, including Antwerp, Ghent and Leuven, and across Europe: I have played in France, Luxembourg, Greece and Austria. For events abroad, travel and accommodation are added to the quote.

How far in advance should we book?

For end-of-year season (November–December), 2–3 months ahead is wise. For other dates, 4–6 weeks is usually comfortable, but ask anyway; last-minute solutions are often possible.
