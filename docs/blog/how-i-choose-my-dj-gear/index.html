How I choose my DJ gear — and why it matters to you

You do not need to speak gearhead to hire well — but knowing why a pro carries what he carries will help you ask the right questions.

Clients rarely ask me about equipment, and honestly, they should not have to. My job is to make the technical side invisible. But every choice in my flight cases exists for a reason that directly affects your event — so let me open the cases and explain, in plain language, what a professional carries and why.

The decks: reliability first, features second

I play on industry-standard club gear — the kind you would find in any serious venue from Brussels to Mykonos. Not because of brand loyalty, but because of two boring words: reliability and familiarity. When I did full summer seasons on Mykonos, playing six nights a week, I learned that gear which survives heat, humidity and eight-hour sets is gear you can trust with someone's wedding. And familiarity matters more than people think: on your event night I want my hands to know every button blind, so my head is free to watch your dance floor.

Everything is also redundant. Two players minimum, music on multiple drives, backup cables for every cable. In years of events I have needed the backups a handful of times — and each time, nobody in the room ever knew.

Sound: the right size, not the biggest size

This is the part I wish every client knew. Good event sound is not about power; it is about matching the system to the room. For a corporate reception of eighty people, I bring a compact system that delivers warm, clear sound at conversation-friendly levels. For a Greek party of four hundred, I bring subwoofers, because a zeibekiko without low end is a crime. My full packages scale up to around 500 guests precisely because I own systems in several sizes rather than forcing one rig onto every event.

What should you check as a client? Three things:

  1. Ask if the DJ scales the system to your venue and guest count. "I always bring the same setup" is the wrong answer.
  2. Ask about speech clarity. Speakers positioned for music are not automatically positioned for a best man's toast. A pro thinks about both.
  3. Ask about volume management. You want a floor that is loud and a bar area where guests can actually talk. That is a setup decision, made before the first guest arrives.

Microphones: the most underrated gear on the list

I carry professional wireless microphones because speeches are, emotionally, the most important audio of many events. A crackling mic during a father's speech is unforgivable, and entirely avoidable. Fresh batteries, tested frequencies, a spare handheld — it is unglamorous and it is essential.

Lighting: atmosphere, not airport runway

Lighting is where taste matters most. My rule: light the mood, not the ceiling. Warm ambient tones during dinner, colour and movement when the dancing starts, and nothing that strobes across grandma's dessert. During my residency at Papillon Schuman in Brussels I learned how much a room changes when lighting follows the arc of the evening instead of blasting from minute one — a lesson I now carry into every corporate event and wedding.

What this means for your budget

Professional gear is a large part of why professional DJs cost what they cost. Insurance, maintenance, transport, backups — none of it is visible on the night, all of it is why the night works. When you compare quotes, you are partly comparing what happens when something goes wrong. With a pro, the answer is: nothing you will ever notice.
