How to choose a wedding DJ in Brussels

What I would look for if I were hiring a DJ for my own wedding, from someone who has stood behind the decks at a few hundred of them.

Every couple I meet asks me some version of the same question: how do we know we are choosing the right DJ? It is a fair worry. Your caterer can be slightly off and nobody remembers. If the music is wrong, everybody remembers. After years of playing weddings across Belgium, and long summer seasons in Mykonos before that, here is the advice I would give my own friends.

Start with fit, not price

The first filter is simple: does this person play the kind of wedding you are having? A DJ who lives on club bookings will struggle with a room that spans three generations and two languages. A wedding DJ Brussels couples can rely on has to read a floor with grandparents, colleagues, teenagers and a table of friends who only want reggaeton, often all at once, and often in two or three languages within the same hour. Brussels weddings are rarely monocultural, and that is exactly what makes them wonderful when the music bridges the room instead of dividing it.

Ask to hear mixes, yes, but also ask how they handled a specific situation: a dinner that ran ninety minutes late, a first dance the couple changed that morning, a room that would not warm up. The answers tell you more than any showreel.

Meet before you book

I usually want a call or a coffee before anything is signed. It is the fastest way for both of us to see whether the evening you have in mind is the one I would play. You are not just buying four hours of music; you are handing someone the emotional steering wheel of your evening. If the conversation feels like a sales pitch rather than a planning session, keep looking.

Questions worth asking

  1. Who exactly will play on the night? Some agencies sell a name and send whoever is free. Book the person you spoke to.
  2. What equipment do you bring, and is there a backup? A professional carries spare cables, a spare player and a plan B for everything.
  3. How do you handle our must-play and never-play lists? The right answer is "religiously".
  4. Do you provide microphones for speeches? Microphones and someone managing them are part of the job, not an extra favour.
  5. What happens if the timeline slips? It always slips. You want someone who shrugs and adapts.
  6. Are travel, setup and breakdown included in the quote? Compare total prices, not headline rates.

Red flags I would run from

  • No written contract or vague terms about overtime.
  • A DJ who talks only about their taste, never about your guests.
  • Refusal to take requests as a blanket policy: filtering requests is craft, refusing them is ego.
  • Suspiciously cheap offers. Someone has to pay for insurance, gear and preparation; if they are not, you are.

The Brussels angle

One practical thing couples underestimate: venues here vary enormously, from château salons in Brabant to industrial lofts in Anderlecht to marquees in a family garden. Each needs different sound. Ask whether your DJ has played your venue or one like it, and whether they scale their sound and light setup to the room rather than bringing the same rig everywhere. In my experience, a correctly sized system at moderate volume beats an oversized one at painful volume every single time, your older guests will thank you at midnight when they are still on the dance floor.

Trust the conversation

In the end, choose the DJ who asks you the most questions. The one who wants to know how you met, which song makes your mother cry, and what your friends danced to at the last wedding you loved. That curiosity is what turns a playlist into your evening.
