A Greek winter under the sheds of Tour & Taxis

The Brussels Greek Food Festival came back for a Winter Edition — and moved into one of the city's most iconic venues.

In June we filled a square in Saint-Gilles. From 29 November to 1 December 2024, the Brussels Greek Food Festival returned with a Winter Edition — this time inside the historic sheds of Tour & Taxis. Trading open sky for iron beams and brick, and summer heat for coats draped over chairs, the question was obvious: does a Greek festival still feel Greek in a Belgian winter?

Short answer: more than ever.

A different room, a different craft

Playing an indoor venue of that scale is a completely different discipline from a summer square. Tour & Taxis has volume — high ceilings, long sightlines, sound that travels. My soundcheck was about taming the space: keeping the low end warm without letting it wash over the conversations at the long tables, because at a food festival the tables are the point. People come to eat, drink and talk — and then, when the music earns it, to dance.

The arc of a winter festival day

Each day had its own shape. Lunchtime crowds wanted atmosphere: rebetiko-flavoured warmth, nostalgic laika, the kind of set I refine every week as a restaurant and lounge DJ. As the afternoon light faded behind the shed windows, I pushed the tempo. By Saturday evening the space in front of the booth had stopped pretending to be a walkway and become a dancefloor.

Winter outside, Aegean inside. That was the brief I gave myself, and the crowd delivered the rest.

Moments I will remember

  • A kalamatianos line snaking between the food stalls on Saturday night, growing as it went, collecting dancers like a magnet collects keys.
  • The stallholders — after two long days on the grills — dancing behind their counters to the closing set on Sunday.
  • Belgian visitors asking me to write down song titles. That is the diaspora doing its quiet work: one melody at a time.

Two editions in, one lesson

Hosting both the summer and winter editions in the same year taught me that a festival is not a venue or a season — it is a community deciding to gather. My job as the festival DJ is to give that gathering a heartbeat: to know when the room wants Zorba and when it wants space to talk over a glass of Assyrtiko.

Congratulations again to the organisers for the leap to Tour & Taxis — an ambitious move that paid off. And to everyone who danced between the stalls: you made a Belgian winter weekend feel like an August panigiri.

If you are planning a festival, a food event or any large-scale Greek party that needs a DJ who understands crowds and cuisine alike, send me a message — I would love to hear about it.
